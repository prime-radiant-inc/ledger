# Rollup usability test — spike v4, rev-12 curation model

2026-08-14. Eight fresh agents — six Sonnet, two Haiku — against spike v4 (spike v3 plus the
rev-12 roll-up model: in-stream `rollup` events with explicit child id lists, two-step
`rollup` verb, `tail` as roots view with `--raw`/`--in`, advisory `rollup_due`). Scenario
substrate: a 23-event two-investigator 504 investigation with distinct finished threads
(repro, refuted hypothesis, confirmed root cause, fix design) and live work (open impl task,
handoff), generated by `spike4/preload.sh`; one copy carried a deliberately planted bad
rollup whose line claims the refuted cache theory was "confirmed as root cause."

## Result: eight for eight, zero tool errors

Every agent completed its scenario. No agent hit a single tool error — not one, across
~40 rollup-touching commands. (The error surface — `child_taken`, `unknown_event`,
multi-line refusal — was verified by the pre-eval smoke test; the agents simply never
made those mistakes.) The zsh-alias trap and wrong-cwd trap from prior rounds appeared
once each and were self-diagnosed; both already have fixes or doc lines in the Go tool.

## What each seat proved

- **Sonnet curator** (23 events → 6 roots): three thread-shaped rollups mapping exactly to
  the investigation's semantic threads, live work deliberately left unrolled, spine verified
  untouched afterward. Made one genuine judgment call the doctrine doesn't cover: a handoff
  note bridging two threads (closing one, opening the next) — anchored it to the thread the
  note's forward-looking payload served. Flagged it as a coin-flip for future curators.
- **Haiku curator** (23 events → 5 roots): also clean, slightly coarser grouping (merged the
  repro thread into the cache rollup). A small model handled orientation, thread selection,
  multi-id submission, and verification (`--in` spot-check) without a stumble.
- **Corrector** — the planted-bad-rollup probe, and the round's most important result: the
  agent drilled the suspect rollup, found the summary ("cache stampede theory confirmed as
  root cause") contradicting every record inside it plus the live `hyp-cache=refuted` spine
  row, and applied the correction idiom exactly as designed — rolled the bad rollup under a
  corrected, evidence-grounded line, changed no status, deleted nothing. **Verify-before-
  trust extends to second-order testimony with zero additional machinery.** Also surfaced
  one initially-confusing-but-correct behavior: `rollup_due` doesn't move on a correction
  (corrections don't reduce loose records).
- **Live worker**: tracked three real tasks with commit evidence, recognized "all tasks
  done" as the doctrine's natural pause, rolled all seven events into one line, closed
  shipped, `rollup_due` 7 → 0. Doctrine timing ("natural pauses, before handoff, at close,
  never mid-flow") mapped onto real work without interpretation trouble.
- **Three cold readers, three reading strategies, three accurate briefings.** Sonnet on the
  Sonnet-curated ledger drilled 2 of 3 rollups and took the third on trust ("its summary
  matched the spine row closely enough"). Sonnet on the *Haiku*-curated ledger drilled all
  3 because its questions demanded verbatim detail; every summary "accurately previewed"
  its contents. Haiku on the Sonnet-curated ledger never drilled at all — it answered
  everything from `show`'s spine plus full-text notes. All five briefing questions answered
  correctly by all three, including the evidence-vs-testimony classification. No reader was
  misled by any summary line, in either cross-model direction.

## Design findings (feed the implementation plan)

1. **Roots ordering needs fixing — the round's one real defect.** Rollups render at their
   own chain position (creation time), so after curation the open `task-fix-impl` appears
   *before* the rollups that causally precede it. The Sonnet curator called it out
   explicitly. Spec amendment: the roots view orders each root by the chain position of its
   earliest transitive base event, so curated history reads in causal order.
2. **Duel fold rule needs one line of spec.** The spike implements all-or-nothing (a rollup
   with *any* already-taken child is wholly inert); the spec's current wording implies
   per-record resolution, which would let a losing rollup's summary line stand while
   covering only part of what it describes. All-or-nothing is right — a summary is a claim
   about its whole child set. Reconcile the spec text.
3. **Evidence adjacency can mislead.** The sharpest reader observation: a summary line that
   bundles an evidence ref into the same sentence as a verdict ("cache stampede theory
   refuted — hit rate 92%+, file:graphs/…") can make an *unevidenced* verdict row look
   evidenced; the reader caught it only in the raw chain. Doctrine tweak for the bare-
   `rollup` instructions: name whose evidence backs what, or keep the anchor next to the
   claim it actually attaches to. Wording, not machinery.
4. **Bridge-note convention.** One doctrine line settles the curator's coin-flip: a note
   that closes one thread and opens another belongs to the thread it opens.
5. **The spine stays the fast path for state questions** — the Haiku reader's no-drill
   route worked *because* curation leaves the fold untouched. This validates the register
   separation: rollups are narrative machinery; nothing state-shaped got worse. It also
   means the MemWalker drill-down-fragility risk barely applies: weak models can route
   around navigation entirely when the spine is healthy.
6. **`show`'s truncated note previews** pushed two readers to `notes`/`tail --in` for full
   text — documented behavior working as designed, worth one quickstart clause so readers
   go straight there.

## Verdict

The rev-12 model is validated at spike fidelity: explicit thread-shaped child selection is
natural for both model classes, the correction idiom works cold against a real planted
error, curation compresses 23 events to 5–6 roots without a single misleading summary in
either cross-model direction, and the advisory-debt model produced rolling at exactly the
doctrinally right moments. Two spec amendments (roots ordering, all-or-nothing duels) and
two doctrine wording tweaks (evidence adjacency, bridge notes) go into the implementation
plan; nothing in the round argues against the design.
