# Academic literature survey: hierarchical summary rollups over append-only logs

Context: evaluating an OptMem-style design (binary merge tree of one-line LLM summaries over an append-only log; read-time detail decay with recent entries verbatim and old ranges collapsed, under a fixed line budget; agent-as-compressor; drill-down into any summary node's two halves) for our ledger CLI.

Surveyed 2026-08-14. arXiv links are given as ids; all are at `https://arxiv.org/abs/<id>`.

---

## 1. Hierarchical / recursive summarization

### RAPTOR — Sarthi, Abdullah, Tuli, Khanna, Goldie, Manning (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2401.18059)

The closest published system to a summary merge tree. RAPTOR builds a tree bottom-up over a document corpus: chunk into ~100-token leaves, embed (SBERT), cluster with a Gaussian mixture model over UMAP-reduced embeddings (soft clustering, so a chunk can join multiple clusters), summarize each cluster with an LLM, re-embed the summaries, and recurse until clustering stops. Retrieval has two modes: **tree traversal** (start at the root, descend into best-matching children) and **collapsed tree** (flatten all nodes from all levels into one pool and take top-k by cosine similarity within a token budget). The collapsed tree consistently won in their ablations. RAPTOR set SOTA at the time on QuALITY, and improved NarrativeQA and QASPER, with the biggest gains on questions requiring multi-step or thematic reasoning that no single chunk answers.

Two findings matter most for us:

1. **Collapsed-tree > traversal.** Matching a query at the "right" abstraction level directly beats being forced to route down through ancestors. Their explanation: questions vary in granularity, and traversal locks you into retrieving a fixed proportion of high- vs low-level nodes. Lesson: OptMem-style drill-down (root → children → grandchildren) is exactly tree traversal; if we ever add *search* over the ledger, we should search all levels at once rather than navigate. For pure context assembly (no query), this doesn't apply, but it warns against making navigation the only access path.
2. **Measured hallucination in summary nodes was small and mostly non-propagating.** Their appendix analysis found roughly 4% of sampled summary nodes contained a hallucination (usually minor extrapolation from the children), and these rarely surfaced in final QA answers. Summary-of-summary hallucination is real but was not catastrophic at 2–3 levels of height.

Note a structural difference: RAPTOR clusters by *semantic similarity*, so siblings are topically coherent. OptMem's binary merge tree groups by *temporal adjacency* (log position). For a chronological event ledger, temporal grouping is arguably right (the log's meaning is its ordering), but expect summaries of temporally-adjacent-but-topically-mixed ranges to be harder to write well — that's the exact problem MemTree and SiReRAG (below) poke at.

### Recursively Summarizing Books with Human Feedback — Wu, Ouyang, Ziegler, Stiennon, Lowe, Leike, Christiano (OpenAI, 2021, arXiv:2109.10862)

The canonical recursive-decomposition summarization paper. Fine-tuned GPT-3 summarizes fixed-size sections of a book, then recursively summarizes concatenated summaries up the tree, with humans giving feedback only on local tasks (summarize this section given prior context). This is the same *task decomposition* OptMem uses: each summary is written from a bounded window (two children), never from the whole history.

Key findings for us: quality degrades with tree height — full-book (depth-3) summaries only occasionally reached human quality (a small minority got high ratings from labelers), and the paper explicitly documents that **important details get lost and errors compound as you go up the tree**, because a higher-level summarizer can only work with what lower levels preserved ("auto-induced distributional shift": upper levels consume model-written, sometimes-flawed inputs). Lesson: treat every summary level as lossy and keep the raw log reachable (OptMem's drill-down does this); expect the root-level line of a large ledger to be thematic at best, and don't let any workflow depend on top-level summaries containing specific facts.

### BooookScore — Chang, Lo, Goyal, Iyyer (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.00785)

First systematic coherence study of the two book-length summarization workflows: **hierarchical merging** (summarize chunks, merge summaries pairwise/groupwise — i.e., the merge-tree shape) vs **incremental updating** (maintain one running summary, fold each new chunk into it — i.e., the "rolling summary" shape many agent frameworks use). 1,193 human annotations over GPT-4 summaries of 100 books; taxonomy of eight coherence error types (entity omission, causal omission, salience errors, etc.).

Result: **hierarchical merging produces more coherent summaries; incremental updating produces more detail but significantly more coherence errors** and tends to bloat/distort as the running summary is repeatedly rewritten. Lesson: this is direct evidence for OptMem's merge-tree over the naive alternative (one continuously-rewritten state summary). Each summary in a merge tree is written once from stable inputs and never rewritten, which avoids the repeated-rewrite degradation that incremental updating suffers. For our ledger this is the strongest single argument for rollup-by-merging rather than rollup-by-rewriting.

### MemWalker — Chen, Pasunuru, Weston, Celikyilmaz (Meta, 2023, arXiv:2310.05029)

"Walking Down the Memory Maze": builds a summary tree over a long text (segments → summary nodes → recursive summaries to a root), then treats QA as **interactive navigation**: the LLM reads child summaries, reasons about which branch to enter, descends, and can back out (they carry a small working memory across steps). This is the published version of OptMem's drill-down loop. Findings: beats retrieval and long-context baselines on QuALITY/SummScreenFD/GovReport *when the underlying model is strong*; navigation accuracy collapses with weaker models because each step is a reasoning decision, and wrong turns compound. Lesson: drill-down works, but (a) summary nodes must be written to support *routing* (what's in this range, distinguishably from its sibling), not just gist — a one-line summary is a signpost first and a summary second; (b) budget for navigation errors: allow backtracking and make opening a node cheap.

### MemTree — Rezazadeh, Li, Wei, Lin, Rajmohan (Accenture; ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.14052)

Online, dynamic tree memory for conversations: each new item is inserted by descending the tree along semantic similarity; parent nodes hold LLM-written aggregated summaries plus embeddings, and abstraction increases toward the root. Node summaries are *updated* when new content is inserted beneath them. Beats flat memory baselines on multi-session dialogue and approaches RAPTOR on document QA while being online rather than batch. Lesson: the main design contrast with OptMem — MemTree organizes by topic and mutates node summaries; OptMem organizes by time and writes each summary once. MemTree shows the semantic-tree alternative is viable, at the cost of update-in-place (which BooookScore's incremental-updating result warns degrades text over many rewrites) and loss of chronology. For an audit-flavored ledger, time-ordered + write-once is the better fit; MemTree is the design to steal from if users later want topical views.

### ReadAgent — Lee, Chen, Furuta, Canny, Fischer (Google DeepMind, 2024, arXiv:2402.09727)

Human-inspired "gist memory": the LLM decides episode boundaries (where to cut the stream into pages), compresses each episode into a short gist, works from the sequence of gists, and **looks up the original raw pages** when a task needs detail. 3–20x effective context extension on QuALITY/NarrativeQA/QMSum, beating retrieval baselines. Two lessons: (1) letting the model choose *semantic* episode boundaries beat fixed-size chunking — OptMem's strict power-of-two merge boundaries are semantically arbitrary and will sometimes split an episode across two summary nodes; worth considering merge boundaries aligned to session/task boundaries. (2) Gist + raw-lookup (exactly summary + drill-down) is validated as the read pattern; the gist alone was not enough.

### Incremental/streaming summarization (brief)

- **Incremental Update Summarization** (McCreadie, Macdonald, Ounis, CIKM 2014) — pre-LLM formulation: emit novel updates from a news stream over time, optimizing novelty + coverage under redundancy constraints. Relevant framing: an append-only summary stream, not a rewritten state.
- **Incremental Summarization for Customer Support via Progressive Note-Taking** (arXiv:2510.06677) — generates summary bullets only when substantial new info appears, with a filter for redundant bullets; production system. Lesson: gate summarization on information gain, not just on count thresholds — a run of 8 trivial ledger events may not deserve the same summary spend as 8 pivotal ones.
- **Context-Aware Hierarchical Merging for Long Document Summarization** (arXiv:2502.00977) — improves hierarchical merging by injecting source-document context during merges, reducing unsupported content in merged summaries. Lesson: when merging two child summaries, giving the summarizer *some* underlying raw lines (not just the two summaries) measurably improves faithfulness — cheap to do in our tool since we own the log.

## 2. LLM agent memory architectures

### MemGPT — Packer, Fang, Patil, Lin, Wooders, Gonzalez (2023, arXiv:2310.08560)

The OS analogy: context window = RAM, external stores = disk, and the LLM manages its own paging via function calls (self-editing core memory, searching recall/archival storage). Two mechanisms map to our design: (1) **eviction with recursive summarization** — when context pressure hits, evicted message history is folded into a summary that stays in context while the raw messages remain queryable in the recall store; (2) **memory-pressure interrupts** — the system, not the model's whim, triggers consolidation. Lessons: the tool should own the trigger (OptMem's synchronous "write this summary now" prompt is the same move — the tool forces consolidation at merge boundaries rather than hoping the agent volunteers), and evicted-but-retrievable is the right contract: summaries in view, raw log always searchable. MemGPT is also the cautionary tale for agent-managed memory: later evaluations (including the Letta team's own) found agents frequently misuse free-form memory tools; OptMem's rigid structure (the tool decides *what* gets summarized *when*; the agent only writes the text) removes most of that failure surface.

### Generative Agents — Park, O'Brien, Cai, Morris, Liang, Bernstein (UIST 2023, arXiv:2304.03442)

The memory stream + reflection architecture. Memory is an **append-only stream** of natural-language observations. Retrieval scores every record as a weighted sum of **recency** (exponential decay over game time), **importance** (LLM-assigned 1–10 at write time), and **relevance** (embedding similarity to the current situation). **Reflection**: when the summed importance of recent events crosses a threshold (150 in the paper), the agent generates ~3 salient questions about recent memory, retrieves evidence for each, and synthesizes higher-level insights *with explicit citations to the evidence records*; reflections are appended to the same stream and can themselves be reflected on, yielding a **tree of increasingly abstract reflections whose leaves are raw observations**. Ablations showed removing reflection measurably degraded agent believability on tasks requiring inference over accumulated experience.

Lessons, in order of importance:
1. **Summaries that cite their sources.** Reflection nodes pointing at evidence records is the paper's most transferable mechanism and is what makes drill-down *trustworthy*: our summary nodes get their child ranges for free (a merge node covers lines i–j), but the same idea argues each summary line should preserve concrete anchors (ids, filenames, numbers) so the reader knows where to drill.
2. **Importance-weighted, not purely positional, decay.** OptMem's decay is purely positional (old = compressed). Generative Agents scores importance per record at write time; a design where high-importance events resist compression (get quoted verbatim inside ancestor summaries, or pinned outside the tree) addresses the obvious failure where a critical old decision ends up as one word in a great-grandparent summary.
3. **Reflection is query-driven synthesis, not mechanical compression** — "what questions would I ask of this period?" produced better abstractions than "shorten this text." Worth stealing for our summary prompt.

### MemoryBank — Zhong, Guo, Gao, Ye, Wang (AAAI 2024, arXiv:2305.10250)

Long-term companion-chatbot memory: hierarchical store (turn-level logs → daily event summaries → global user summary/personality portrait) plus a forgetting mechanism explicitly modeled on the **Ebbinghaus curve**: each memory has a strength; retrieval "rehearses" it, resetting/flattening its decay; unrehearsed memories are eventually dropped. Lesson: the one widely-cited system where *access refreshes retention*. For a ledger, actual deletion is wrong, but the rehearsal idea transfers to the read path: entries (or subtrees) the agent repeatedly drills into are demonstrably still load-bearing, and a rendering policy could keep hot subtrees at higher resolution within the same line budget instead of decaying purely by age.

### HippoRAG — Gutiérrez, Shu, Gu, Yasunaga, Su (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.14831)

Models the hippocampal indexing theory: an LLM extracts an entity/relation knowledge graph (the "hippocampal index") over a passage corpus (the "neocortex"), and retrieval runs Personalized PageRank from query entities over the graph to select passages. Big multi-hop QA gains at 10–30x lower cost than iterative retrieval. Lesson for us is architectural: the index is a *separate, derived, lossy* structure whose only job is to route to intact raw content — it never replaces the content. That's the same invariant OptMem keeps (summaries route, log persists), and HippoRAG is the evidence that single-pass "connect new experience into an index at write time" can substitute for expensive multi-step read-time reasoning.

### A-Mem — Xu, Liang, Mei, Gao, Tan, Zhang (2025, arXiv:2502.12110)

Zettelkasten-style agentic memory: each new memory becomes a structured note (context, keywords, tags); the system links it to related existing notes and — notably — **updates old notes' descriptions in light of new ones** ("memory evolution"). Strong multi-hop results with 85–93% lower memory-operation token cost than baselines. Lesson: the graph/network alternative to a tree; its memory-evolution step is the opposite of our append-only philosophy and reintroduces the rewrite-degradation risk, but it names a real gap in pure chronological rollups: connections between distant log ranges ("this reverts the decision from three weeks ago") are invisible to a merge tree. A cheap borrow: let the summarizing agent reference earlier ledger line-ids inside summary text.

### Mem0 — Chhikara, Khant, Aryan, Singh, Yadav (2025, arXiv:2504.19413)

Production memory pipeline: an extraction stage pulls candidate facts from the dialogue; an update stage compares each against existing memories and picks ADD / UPDATE / DELETE / NOOP; a graph variant (Mem0g) stores entity-relations. Reports 26% relative quality gain over the OpenAI memory baseline on LOCOMO with 91% lower p95 latency and >90% token savings versus full-context. Lesson: strongest published evidence that *structured consolidated memory beats replaying the full raw history* on both quality and cost for long conversations — i.e., the rollup is not just a context-budget workaround, it can improve task performance. Its fact-level CRUD is the "distill into atomic facts" alternative to our "compress the narrative in place" approach.

### Consolidation-flavored 2025–26 work (brief)

- **Sleep-time Compute** — Lin, Snell, Wang, Packer, Wooders, Stoica, Gonzalez (Letta, 2025, arXiv:2504.13171): spend offline compute pre-processing context (anticipating queries, precomputing inferences) before queries arrive; ~5x test-time compute reduction at equal accuracy, amortized across queries on the same context. Design-relevant tension: OptMem writes summaries **synchronously** at append time (blocking the agent mid-task); this line of work argues consolidation is naturally an *idle-time/background* activity. A background or end-of-session rollup pass is the alternative to weigh against OptMem's synchronous prompt.
- **TiMem** (arXiv:2601.02845): temporal-hierarchical memory tree for conversational agents — raw observations at leaves, progressively refined higher-level representations, semantic-guided consolidation across levels; cut recalled-memory length by ~52% on LoCoMo while beating baselines. Independent recent validation of the exact shape we're evaluating (time-ordered leaves, hierarchical consolidation, big read-time token savings).
- **Toward a Theory of Hierarchical Memory for Language Agents** (arXiv:2603.21564): first attempt at a shared formalism — decomposes systems into extraction, **coarsening** (their name for our rollup), and traversal operators, and shows a coupling: how self-sufficient a node's representation is constrains which retrieval strategies can work. Useful vocabulary: OptMem chooses maximal coarsening with traversal-only access, which by their analysis demands highly self-sufficient (information-dense, routing-capable) node summaries.
- **Surveys**: Zhang, Bo, Ma et al., *A Survey on the Memory Mechanism of LLM-based Agents* (2024, arXiv:2404.13501; ACM TOIS 2025) — taxonomy along representation (textual/parametric), lifecycle (writing, managing, reading), and the sources of memory; good map of the space. *From Human Memory to AI Memory* (2025, arXiv:2504.15965) — bridges psychology categories (episodic/semantic/working) to LLM memory designs. Both classify hierarchical summarization as one of three main "memory management" strategies alongside selective forgetting and structured (graph/DB) storage.

## 3. Cognitive science grounding (brief)

The design has a clean analog in **complementary learning systems / systems consolidation** (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995, *Psychological Review* 102(3); updated in Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016, *TiCS*): the hippocampus stores sparse, detailed episodic traces fast; slow replay-driven consolidation extracts statistical structure ("gist") into neocortex, while — per the multiple-trace/trace-transformation view — detailed episodic traces are retained alongside the gist rather than overwritten. The mapping: raw ledger = hippocampal episodes, rollup summaries = neocortical gist, and the biology's vote is firmly for *keeping both* (which drill-down does) rather than replace-on-consolidate. Consolidation in the brain is also an **offline** (sleep/replay) process, which weakly supports background rather than synchronous summarization. **Chunking** (Miller 1956; Gobet et al. 2001, *TiCS*) is the standard account of why hierarchical grouping extends effective capacity: experts recode many items into one label they can later unpack — precisely a summary node — and it works because chunks are *meaningful* units, again suggesting semantic rather than purely positional merge boundaries.

On decay: **Ebbinghaus (1885)** gives forgetting as roughly power-law/exponential in time, and **Anderson & Schooler (1991, *Psychological Science* 2(6))** showed ACT-R's base-level activation — decay by time since use, boosted by frequency and recency of *access* — mirrors the actual probability an item will be needed again. That's the strongest cognitive argument that decay should be a function of (age, access history), not age alone. **Soar's episodic memory** (Nuxoll & Laird, ICCM 2004 / AAAI 2007) is the append-only precedent: automatic, unmodified snapshots taken every decision cycle, temporally indexed, never rewritten — while Derbinsky & Laird's forgetting work (Cognitive Systems Research 2013) removes only knowledge that is inactive *and reconstructible*, a good rule for what a rollup may safely elide: drop from view what drill-down can reconstruct, never what it can't.

## 4. Theory: budgeted variable-resolution coverage of an append-only sequence

Nothing formalizes the exact "LLM-summary merge tree" problem, but the *structure* is well studied:

### Exponential histograms — Datar, Gionis, Indyk & Motwani (SODA 2002 / SIAM J. Comput. 31(6): 1794–1813)

The canonical formalization of "cover a stream's recent history with a budget of variable-resolution blocks." To count over sliding windows, DGIM cover the stream suffix with **buckets whose sizes are powers of two, growing exponentially with age**: the most recent items sit in size-1 buckets; when more than k buckets of a size accumulate, the two oldest merge into one of double size. This maintains any suffix count within relative error ε using O((1/ε)·log N) buckets — and they prove a matching Ω((1/ε)·log²N)-bit **lower bound**: you cannot do asymptotically better. The merge rule is *identical in shape* to OptMem's binary merge tree with read-time decay (recent = fine buckets, old = exponentially coarser buckets, fixed budget). The transferable results: (a) a logarithmic budget suffices for exponentially growing history — an L-line rendering budget covers a ~2^O(L) line log; (b) exponential bucket growth is *optimal* if the fidelity you want is "uniform relative error over every suffix," i.e., the most recent fraction of history always matters most — which is exactly the right assumption for an agent's working ledger; (c) **Braverman & Ostrovsky's smooth histograms** (FOCS 2007) generalize the guarantee to any "smooth" function, showing the framework's condition: the technique works when the summarized quantity degrades gracefully under merging. LLM summary *quality* has no such smoothness guarantee — that's precisely the gap between the theory and OptMem (see §5).

### Related structural theory

- **Bentley & Saxe, the logarithmic method** (*J. Algorithms* 1(4), 1980): the static-to-dynamic transformation maintains an append-only set as a forest of power-of-two-sized static blocks, merging blocks like binary-counter carries — amortized O(log N) merge work per insertion. OptMem's merge cadence *is* Bentley–Saxe; the amortized bound tells us summary-writing cost per ledger append is O(log N) summary invocations amortized, with occasional O(N)-range merges (the carry cascade) — an argument for making big merges asynchronous so a single append doesn't stall on rewriting the root.
- **LSM-trees** (O'Neil, Cheng, Gawlick, O'Neil, *Acta Informatica* 33, 1996) are the systems instantiation: append-optimized levels of exponentially increasing size with background compaction. The whole LSM literature (compaction scheduling, write amplification, tiering vs leveling) is a design vocabulary for "when do merges run and who pays" — directly reusable for scheduling summary rewrites.
- **Dyadic decomposition / segment trees / wavelet-based histograms**: any contiguous range [i, j] of a length-N sequence is exactly covered by ≤ 2·log N dyadic (power-of-two-aligned) nodes; this underlies segment trees, wavelet trees (Grossi, Gupta & Vitter, SODA 2003), and wavelet-based histograms for budgeted approximation (Matias, Vitter & Wang, SIGMOD 1998 — keep the B most important coefficients of a hierarchical decomposition, an early "importance-weighted resolution budget"). For us: a binary merge tree guarantees *any* time range of the ledger is presentable as O(log N) summary nodes — the formal basis for "show me June" costing a logarithmic number of lines.
- **Tilted-time windows** (Chen, Dong, Han, Wah & Wang, VLDB 2002; Giannella, Han, Pei, Yan & Yu's FP-Stream, 2003): stream mining's standard "natural/logarithmic tilted time window" keeps recent data at fine granularity and progressively coarsens older data (last hour by minute, last day by hour, …), with O(log N) total windows. This is read-time detail decay, published 2002, minus the LLM.
- **arXiv:2603.21564** (§2 above) is the only work found that formalizes the *LLM* version (coarsening operators + traversal constraints); it's taxonomy, not theorems with error bounds. The open problem — bounds relating summary budget to downstream task fidelity when the compressor is an LLM — appears genuinely unformalized.

## 5. Known failure modes

1. **Compounding error / detail loss with tree height.** Wu et al. 2021 (arXiv:2109.10862) document it directly: higher-level summarizers can only see what lower levels preserved, errors compound, and depth-3 book summaries only rarely reached human quality. Salience is the core failure: each level makes local keep/drop decisions without knowing what a future reader needs. Mitigations seen in the literature: keep raw text reachable (ReadAgent, RAPTOR, MemGPT — all do), pass some raw context into merges (arXiv:2502.00977), and cite evidence downward (Generative Agents).
2. **Hallucination in summaries-of-summaries.** Baseline abstractive summarizers hallucinate substantially (Maynez et al., *On Faithfulness and Factuality in Abstractive Summarization*, ACL 2020 — majority of generated summaries contained unsupported content with pre-LLM models); with modern LLMs RAPTOR measured only ~4% of summary nodes affected, mostly benign, and limited propagation into answers. Risk is real but low per node with a strong model; it compounds multiplicatively with height, which argues for keeping trees shallow-ish (large branching or capped depth) and for grounding merges in raw lines, not only child summaries.
3. **Incremental rewriting degrades text.** BooookScore (arXiv:2310.00785): the running-summary pattern (rewrite state on every chunk) yields more coherence errors than merge-based hierarchy. Repeated LLM rewriting of the same artifact drifts. Write-once merge nodes dodge this; any feature that "refreshes" old summaries in place (MemTree, A-Mem style) re-imports the risk.
4. **Summary-only indexes lose retrieval performance on detail questions.** Multi-view indexing ablations (arXiv:2404.15103) show summary views and raw-text views each contribute independently — dropping either hurts (summary view most, raw view second). RAPTOR's collapsed-tree result says the same: access to *all* resolution levels beats any single level. SiReRAG (arXiv:2412.06206) further shows similarity-organized trees and relatedness-organized structure capture different questions. Lesson: never make the rendered rollup the only read path; keep grep/drill-down over raw entries first-class.
5. **Navigation fragility.** MemWalker (arXiv:2310.05029): drill-down is a chain of LLM routing decisions; weak models take wrong branches and error compounds down the path. One-line summaries doing double duty as routing signposts is the load-bearing assumption of the whole OptMem design — summary-writing prompts should optimize for discriminability between siblings, and evaluation of our tool should test "can the agent find X by drilling?" not just "is the summary accurate?".

## What this means for our design (synthesis)

- **The shape is sound and independently reinvented everywhere**: exponential detail decay over an append-only log = DGIM buckets (2002), tilted-time windows (2002), Bentley–Saxe merging (1980), TiMem (2026). A logarithmic line budget genuinely covers exponential history, and exponential coarsening is provably optimal for recency-weighted fidelity.
- **Merge-tree (write-once) beats rolling rewrite** — BooookScore is direct evidence; it's also the append-only-friendly choice.
- **Keep the raw log first-class**: every successful system (RAPTOR, ReadAgent, MemGPT, HippoRAG) treats summaries as a routing/gist layer over intact raw content, and cognitive science (CLS theory, Soar) agrees. Drill-down is validated (MemWalker, ReadAgent) with the caveat that summaries must be written as discriminative signposts.
- **Pure positional decay is the design's weakest assumption.** Generative Agents (importance), MemoryBank (rehearsal on access), and ACT-R (base-level activation) all argue retention should factor in importance and access history, not age alone. Cheap fix: importance-tagged ledger events that resist compression (stay verbatim in ancestors or are pinned), and/or letting frequently-drilled subtrees render at higher resolution.
- **Synchronous agent-as-compressor is contrarian**: MemGPT-style system-triggered consolidation supports the tool forcing the write, but sleep-time compute and consolidation biology suggest big merges belong off the critical path. Hybrid: synchronous cheap leaf summaries, background/deferred higher-level merges.
- **Ground merges in raw lines**: when writing a parent summary, include sampled raw entries from the range, not just the two child summaries — measured to reduce unsupported content (arXiv:2502.00977) and it caps compounding error.
